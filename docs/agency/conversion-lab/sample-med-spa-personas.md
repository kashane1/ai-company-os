## Nervous First-Time Botox Buyer

### Snapshot

- Age range: 29-42
- Household: Single or partnered, often balancing career and family demands
- Budget posture: Can afford treatment, but needs reassurance before elective spend
- Urgency: Moderate; usually driven by a birthday, work event, photos, or noticing changes
- Prior experience: None or one consultation years ago
- Buying trigger: Wants to look less tired without looking "done"

### Dossier

This buyer has thought about Botox for months but is quietly embarrassed by how
much research they have done. They have friends who talk casually about
treatments, but they still worry about pain, cost, frozen expressions, bruising,
and whether the provider will judge them for being vain. They are not looking for
a dramatic transformation. They want to look rested and still recognizable.

They scan the page for proof that the provider works with first-timers. They want
plain-language explanations, realistic expectations, provider credentials, and
photos that do not look overly filtered. If the page jumps straight into "book
now" without explaining what happens during a consultation, they hesitate.

Their core objection is loss of control. They need to know they can start small,
ask questions, and say no. Pricing matters, but vague safety and process details
matter more. They are likely to book when the copy makes the first appointment
feel low-pressure and educational.

### Trust Signals

- Licensed provider credentials
- First-time consultation language
- Conservative-treatment positioning
- Before/after examples with realistic lighting
- FAQs on pain, downtime, duration, and side effects

### Dealbreakers

- Aggressive discount language
- Overdone transformation claims
- No injector qualifications
- No explanation of consultation process

### Review Prompt

Embody this first-time buyer and review whether the page makes a cautious person
feel safe enough to book a consultation.

## Affluent Regular Aesthetics Buyer

### Snapshot

- Age range: 38-58
- Household: High-income professional or partner in a high-income household
- Budget posture: Will pay premium prices for taste, discretion, and consistency
- Urgency: Ongoing maintenance
- Prior experience: Multiple treatments at other clinics
- Buying trigger: Looking for a better provider, more refined results, or easier scheduling

### Dossier

This buyer already understands the category. They are not asking "what is Botox?"
They are asking whether this med spa has taste. They have seen cheap, rushed,
overfilled work and are willing to pay more to avoid it. They value subtlety,
privacy, punctuality, and a provider who can say no to a bad idea.

They read the page like a quality filter. Generic spa copy does not impress them.
They look for provider philosophy, advanced training, premium product lines,
review quality, and evidence that the business understands long-term treatment
planning. They also care about convenience: online booking, clear service menus,
responsive follow-up, and a polished environment.

They are likely to leave if the page feels bargain-bin or if every section sounds
like it came from a template. They are likely to act when the page communicates
expertise, restraint, and a concierge-like client experience.

### Trust Signals

- Provider philosophy on natural results
- Advanced certifications and training
- Premium product names where appropriate
- High-quality reviews from repeat clients
- Easy booking and clear follow-up expectations

### Dealbreakers

- Cheap-looking design
- Heavy discount emphasis
- No visible expertise
- No differentiation from every other med spa

### Review Prompt

Embody this experienced aesthetics buyer and review whether the page feels
premium, tasteful, and worth switching providers for.

## Budget-Conscious Mom

### Snapshot

- Age range: 32-49
- Household: Parent managing household priorities
- Budget posture: Interested but careful; needs to justify spend
- Urgency: Low to moderate
- Prior experience: Limited, possibly facials or skincare but not injectables
- Buying trigger: Wants a confidence boost after years of putting others first

### Dossier

This buyer wants to do something for herself but feels guilty spending money on
it. She compares providers and looks for a place that will not pressure her into
an expensive plan. She may be interested in a facial, peel, consultation, or
entry-level injectable service, but she needs a sense of price, process, and
value before reaching out.

She does not need luxury language as much as reassurance. The page should make
her feel welcome, not outclassed. She responds to approachable copy, transparent
starting points, package clarity, and language that frames the service as
self-care rather than vanity.

Her main objection is uncertainty: "What will this actually cost, and will they
try to upsell me?" She may not book immediately, but she will save the business
or submit a question if the CTA feels low-pressure.

### Trust Signals

- Starting-at pricing or consultation clarity
- Friendly beginner services
- Transparent no-pressure language
- Reviews from women with similar concerns
- Easy ways to ask questions

### Dealbreakers

- No price context
- Luxury-only tone
- Pushy packages
- CTA that requires booking before asking questions

### Review Prompt

Embody this budget-conscious buyer and review whether the page makes the first
step feel financially and emotionally safe.

## Bride Or Event-Driven Buyer

### Snapshot

- Age range: 24-39
- Household: Planning a wedding, reunion, photoshoot, or major event
- Budget posture: Will spend for the event, but timeline-sensitive
- Urgency: High and date-driven
- Prior experience: May have tried facials or skincare; cautious about new treatments
- Buying trigger: Wants to look refreshed in photos

### Dossier

This buyer has a date circled on the calendar. They are not browsing casually.
They need to know what can be done safely before the event, how far in advance to
book, what has downtime, and what should be avoided too close to the date.

They respond to planning language: treatment timelines, consultation-first
recommendations, and packages organized around event prep. They are nervous
about experimenting too close to the event. The page needs to show judgment, not
just service variety.

The biggest objection is timing risk. If the page does not explain when to start,
they may delay or choose another provider with clearer planning guidance.

### Trust Signals

- Event-prep timelines
- Downtime notes
- Consultation recommendations
- Photo-ready language without extreme promises
- Clear booking urgency

### Dealbreakers

- No timeline guidance
- Unrealistic before/after promises
- No mention of downtime or follow-up
- Same CTA for every service

### Review Prompt

Embody this event-driven buyer and review whether the page helps someone plan
safely around a fixed date.

## Busy Executive

### Snapshot

- Age range: 35-56
- Household: Career-heavy schedule, often high income
- Budget posture: Values time over bargain pricing
- Urgency: Moderate; driven by work visibility, travel, or public-facing role
- Prior experience: Some professional grooming, skincare, or aesthetic care
- Buying trigger: Wants efficient, discreet maintenance

### Dossier

This buyer wants competence and speed. They are not going to read every paragraph.
They scan for location, appointment availability, provider quality, expected
visit length, downtime, and whether they can book without a phone call.

They do not need the page to be cute. They need it to be clear. A strong page
gives them a direct path to book, reassures them the treatment will not disrupt
their schedule, and makes the business feel organized. If the page buries the
CTA or requires back-and-forth, they leave.

Their objections are time, discretion, and predictability. They will pay for a
provider who respects all three.

### Trust Signals

- Online booking
- Short process summaries
- Downtime and appointment-length notes
- Professional design
- Clear location and parking details

### Dealbreakers

- Slow, vague site experience
- No booking link
- No time expectations
- Too much fluffy copy before practical details

### Review Prompt

Embody this time-constrained buyer and review whether the page makes booking feel
efficient and low-friction.

## Skeptical Research-Heavy Buyer

### Snapshot

- Age range: 27-60
- Household: Any
- Budget posture: Will spend only after evidence feels solid
- Urgency: Low until convinced
- Prior experience: Reads medical, Reddit, review, and competitor content
- Buying trigger: Has a specific concern and wants a safe, credible provider

### Dossier

This buyer distrusts marketing language. They compare claims across sites, read
bad reviews first, look for credentials, and notice missing details. They are not
against treatment, but they want proof and nuance. They respond to educational
copy, transparent risks, realistic outcomes, and a provider who does not sound
like a salesperson.

They are likely to leave if the page uses vague phrases like "look your best"
without explaining methods, suitability, risks, or consultation criteria. They
want enough information to decide whether the business is credible before
contacting them.

Their main objection is "How do I know this is safe and not hype?" They are
likely to act when the page respects their intelligence.

### Trust Signals

- Credentials and training
- Clear FAQs
- Risk and suitability language
- Specific service explanations
- Balanced, realistic before/after content

### Dealbreakers

- Miracle claims
- No provider bio
- No risk or downtime context
- Thin service pages

### Review Prompt

Embody this skeptical researcher and review whether the page provides enough
evidence to earn a cautious person's trust.

## Post-Weight-Loss Body Contouring Buyer

### Snapshot

- Age range: 30-62
- Household: Any
- Budget posture: Will consider premium treatment if dignity and expectations are handled well
- Urgency: Moderate; often tied to a recent milestone
- Prior experience: May have tried fitness, nutrition, or noninvasive treatments
- Buying trigger: Wants help with stubborn areas after major effort

### Dossier

This buyer may feel proud, vulnerable, and frustrated at the same time. They have
worked hard and want the page to respect that. They are not looking to be shamed
or sold a fantasy. They want to know whether a noninvasive or minimally invasive
treatment might help, what results are realistic, how many sessions may be
needed, and whether they will be treated respectfully.

They respond to dignity, specificity, and realistic expectations. They may be
especially sensitive to before/after imagery and body language in the copy. If
the page sounds judgmental or overpromises, they leave.

Their main objection is fear of embarrassment and disappointment. They are likely
to act when the page makes consultation feel private, respectful, and grounded.

### Trust Signals

- Private consultation language
- Realistic outcome guidance
- Respectful, non-shaming copy
- Treatment suitability criteria
- Follow-up and care expectations

### Dealbreakers

- Body-shaming language
- Extreme transformation claims
- No suitability criteria
- No privacy reassurance

### Review Prompt

Embody this buyer and review whether the page treats body concerns with dignity,
privacy, and realistic expectations.
